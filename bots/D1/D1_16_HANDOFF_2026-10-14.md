# D1.16 Development Handoff — 2026-10-14

## Mission

Build **D1.16** from the frozen D1.15 gameplay core and make it competitive with current leading CAMBC submissions.

The first live target is deliberately modest and measurable: win at least **5 of 25 maps (20%)** in a repeat of the five unranked match groups listed below. A 20% result would be a major improvement over D1.15's 0–25. Do not claim success from local proxy matches alone.

The strategic objective is **submitted gem margin**. Camp clears, gold, kills, survival and fuel are useful diagnostic measures only when they improve the chance of submitting more gems than the opponent.

## What the replays established

### 1. There is an independent economy gap

Against Mite on Expanse, D1 lost 722–1,042 with **zero champion kills for either side**. Near round 387, D1 had 990 gold, 722 banked gems and 21 camps; Mite had 1,330 gold, 972 banked gems and 29 camps. D1 also suffered three deaths with no opponent kill credit, consistent with camp or environmental attrition.

This matchup isolates the farming loop. Improving combat avoidance alone cannot close the gap. D1 needs more profitable clears, fewer wasted travel turns, reliable capture completion and safer health management against camps.

### 2. There is a separate combat and routing collapse

Against hello on Collision, D1 lost 88–1,008. Near round 438, D1 trailed 1,450–3,615 in gold, 88–986 in banked gems, 0–12 in champion kills and 21–35 in camps. D1 led 22–0 early, then repeatedly returned to the same losing central interaction.

D1 used Cassi & Issac / Vendetta / Varg. hello used Cassi & Issac / Stellar / Ren. The active D1 policy uses abilities and shields for camps, while enemy handling is mostly an opportunistic basic attack. It lacks projected threat zones, coordinated focus, useful disables, carrier protection and a disengage rule.

### 3. The failures combine on ordinary maps

Against Univardisty on Monolith, near round 406 D1 trailed 264–950 in banked gems, 2,095–4,396 in gold, 22–30 in camps and 0–9 in kills. D1 both farms more slowly and loses contact badly.

### 4. Current leaders use flexible meta rosters

Observed roster families prominently included Cassi & Issac, Stellar and Tavi, plus Varg and Vendetta variants. hello's decisive example used Cassi & Issac / Stellar / Ren. Static map-only drafting leaves information unused because later draft rounds can see the opponent's revealed earlier picks.

## Active D1.15 behavior that must be challenged

1. **Immediate banking:** `simple_policy.py` calls `bank()` and returns whenever the champion carries any positive number of gems. This can turn tiny payouts into long altar trips and reduce camp throughput.
2. **Merged camp goals:** all available non-respawning camp tiles become one bitboard and the pathing core advances toward the closest reachable goal. There is no expected reward, clear time, capture time, return time, ownership or danger term.
3. **Independent action only:** there is no limited regroup for a valuable threatened carrier or contested capture.
4. **Reactive threat detection:** enemies matter mainly after entering current basic attack range. The bot does not score tiles by projected enemy reach or disable range.
5. **PvP abilities absent:** shields, resets and damage abilities are selected around camp clearing rather than enemy fights and exits.
6. **Opponent-blind draft:** `draft.py` uses map signature and side but ignores visible enemy selections.
7. **All Strength shopping:** the bot buys Strength shards only. Do not change this without a champion-specific measured reason; prior broad durability changes lost submitted gems.
8. **No telemetry:** live replay stdout did not expose objective, mode, carried gems, health, target change, retreat reason, capture progress or channel interruption.

## Official rule constraints that matter

- A camp group requires one allied occupant on a spawn tile for a fixed five-round capture. Extra allies do not speed capture and split the reward.
- Leaving the region empty or allowing an enemy into it resets capture progress.
- Captures resolve before tribute channels, so a capture reward can be included in a tribute that completes that round.
- Vision is per champion; shared team memory is only 8 bytes during the game.
- Draft memory is 1,024 bytes. The draft bot sees the full static map and all opponent selections revealed from previous draft rounds.
- Unused fuel does not carry over.
- Python receives 25 times the CLI fuel argument. The competition's 2,500,000 Python fuel limit is tested locally with `--fuel 100000`.
- A fuel suspension can resume next round from the point of suspension, which can quietly destroy action timing even without a crash. Keep per-turn computation bounded.

## Prior experiments that must not be repeated blindly

These results are evidence against the named implementation, not proof that the broad concept can never work.

| Experiment | Result | Lesson for D1.16 |
|---|---|---|
| Full-team rally at 40 gems | 1/8 local pilot; fewer deaths but far fewer submitted gems | Independent farming remains the default. Do not pull the whole team off camps. |
| One rotating escort / altar escort | Mixed local results; failed focused pressure tests | Regroup only for a visible, valuable and endangered carrier, with a strict action budget. |
| Cheap full-cycle geometric camp utility | 3/8 local pilot and severe map regressions | Straight-line estimates and hard target selection can lose tempo. Use real distance data, bounded candidates and soft persistence. |
| Safe 20-gem carry threshold | No broad gain | Do not use one universal bag threshold. Continuing to farm must depend on nearby value, health, route risk and incremental cycle time. |
| Safe return route for bags of 50+ | Lost one win in each tested suite | Broad danger avoidance can add travel without improving conversion. Apply risk costs to a small decision boundary and retain fallback routing. |
| One capturer reservation | More collected gems in places, but fewer submitted and more deaths | Camp ownership can concentrate value on a doomed carrier. Claims must expire and account for loaded owners leaving. |
| Simplified nearest-goal navigation | 1/8 against its parent and a 4,520-gem submission deficit | Preserve the proven native distance-field movement core. Simpler geometry is not automatically more efficient. |
| Higher global or Varg retreat thresholds | Regressed full matrices | Health alone is an inadequate retreat signal. Include bag value, local numbers, incoming threat and route escape. |
| Carrier attack priority without kill certainty | Regressed | Keep the existing lethal-carrier exception. Spend actions on nonlethal pursuit only when denial value is measurable. |

The strongest general lesson from the evolution log is: **maximize expected secured gems per action**. Survival, escorting and denial must repay the gathering actions they consume.

## D1.16 design direction

Build D1.16 as a sequence of small challengers. Do not attempt a large global planner in one step.

### A. Add low-cost decision telemetry first

Make replay diagnosis possible before changing policy. Emit compact, sampled records rather than printing every detail every turn. Suggested fields:

- round, champion ID/type and mode;
- position, health percentage and carried gems;
- selected camp/group and reason for changing it;
- capture owner/progress if visible;
- bank decision and nearest altar distance;
- visible enemy count, nearest threat distance and retreat reason;
- action result and exceptional fallback;
- estimated fuel checkpoint if the SDK exposes one.

Limit output to target changes, mode changes, deaths/respawns, bank starts/completions and perhaps every 20 rounds. Telemetry must not materially consume fuel.

### B. Repair the economy on zero-contact evidence

The first gameplay challenger should target Mite's no-kill Expanse advantage. Preserve D1.15 combat and immediate banking initially so the cause is isolated.

Implement a **bounded reward-aware camp selector**:

1. Precompute static camp groups, group rewards, group bounds and distance fields during the draft or first gameplay turn where practical.
2. On a target change, shortlist only a few reachable candidates using the existing distance-field machinery.
3. Rank the shortlist by expected secured reward divided by estimated remaining turns. Include travel, visible remaining camp health, attack cadence, fixed capture time and altar-return distance.
4. Penalise visible ally duplication, already occupied captures, enemy-side depth and known danger. Keep these as soft terms.
5. Retain a target while it remains valid and competitive, but expire the commitment quickly when the camp is gone, contested, unreachable or much worse than a newly available local option.
6. Keep nearest-reachable goals as the safe fallback when data is incomplete.

Do not reproduce E14's straight-line global scorer. Use actual reachable distance information and compare only a bounded shortlist.

Suggested evidence gate for this phase on Expanse: beat D1.15 on camps captured, gold earned and submitted gems across both sides without raising deaths or producing fuel suspensions. A useful live target is at least 25 camps and 800 submitted gems in a comparable low-contact game; treat those values as direction, not proof from a single match.

### C. Test a narrow carry continuation rule

Only after the camp selector is stable, test whether immediate banking wastes a measurable number of turns. A champion may finish one nearby profitable cycle before returning only when all of the following are true:

- health is safe for the visible camp damage;
- no visible enemy threatens the route or next objective;
- the additional objective is genuinely close by reachable distance;
- the champion is not already close to an unlocked altar;
- the bag and time at risk remain bounded;
- the expected extra secured gems repay the added clear, capture and return time.

Bank immediately when threatened, unhealthy, near an altar, holding a high-value bag, late in the game, or lacking a clearly profitable nearby continuation. Compare this rule to the economy-improved immediate-bank parent. Remove it if it merely increases collected gems or deaths without submitted gems.

### D. Add a conservative contact controller

Represent only a few clear modes: `FARM`, `CAPTURE`, `RETURN`, `EVADE` and, if justified, `SUPPORT`. Recompute the mode from observable local facts rather than following a long plan.

Before stepping into contact, estimate:

- enemies that can attack or disable the destination after their movement;
- nearby allied bodies able to trade;
- current health and shield availability;
- carried gems at risk on both sides;
- whether the route moves deeper into enemy territory or toward an altar/allied rally;
- whether an escape tile remains.

Avoid a locally losing fight when empty handed and farming value is available elsewhere. Protect a loaded champion through local movement, shield, disable or focus fire only when the bag at risk makes the action worthwhile. Never make all champions rally by default.

### E. Give PvP a separate ability policy

Audit the official abilities for the active roster candidates. Add small champion-specific rules for:

- shielding a valuable carrier or self before unavoidable damage;
- using disables to secure an exit or interrupt an enemy carrier;
- focus firing a killable high-value target;
- using movement abilities to escape with gems;
- refusing low-value attacks that delay capture or banking.

Collision against hello is the first screen. The initial goal is to stop the repeat-death loop and raise D1's submitted result substantially above 88. Do not optimize for kill count at the expense of banking.

### F. Use revealed draft information

Keep D1.15's map/side prior, then test changes to **later picks** based on opponent selections from earlier draft rounds. Both teams' picks in a round are revealed together; Team B cannot see Team A's pick for that same round. Mirror picks are legal. Use `cambc.draft.current_draft(cambc.Team.ENEMY)` for revealed picks only.

The draft must fit the gameplay policy that actually runs. D1's active `simple_policy.py` and `combat.py` use damage spells, basic-attack resets, Varg's shield and Tavi's field shopping. They do not currently use dashes for tactical approach or escape, and they do not deliberately use PvP stuns. Therefore **Ren as a ranged-team diver, Vendetta as an assassin, or Marcella as an anti-stun escort are hypotheses, not current capabilities**. A roster change should either gain from an already active passive/action or be paired with a separately measured gameplay change.

#### Draft candidates to test, not automatic counters

| Revealed opponent pattern | Draft-only candidate | Why it may help | Main reason it may fail |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cassi & Issac plus a ranged damage pick such as Stellar | Replace a fragile or redundant later pick with **Varg or Ryth** if D1 has no durable melee farmer; test **Stellar** as a later pick when D1 already has a frontliner | Preserve camp damage while reducing the chance that contact wipes an all-fragile roster | The current bot does not coordinate focus fire; roster durability may simply reduce camp throughput |
| Two revealed stun picks, especially on a compact map with contested altars | Test **Marcella** as one later pick | Her stun immunity is passive and can protect a tribute from stun interruption | She can still be killed or altar-locked; replacing Tavi, Varg or the linked pair may lose too much economy |
| Several melee picks and few ranged threats | Test **Stellar** after a durable farmer is secured | Ranged area and single-target camp damage remain useful even when PvP control is weak | Stellar cannot usually walk and basic-attack on the same turn and is fragile |
| Wide map with many independent camp routes and no early evidence of focused pair hunting | Keep **Cassi & Issac** as the map/side opening candidate | One draft selection creates two acting farmers | Either sibling dying removes both and their bags; the 0–25 live run gives no proof that extra bodies overcome this risk |

Do not infer that a common enemy pick must be countered. The first explicit draft experiment should compare **Collision Team A's current Cassi & Issac / Vendetta / Varg order** with **Cassi & Issac / Stellar / Varg** against a locally built Cassi & Issac / Stellar / Ren roster, using unchanged D1 gameplay. This replaces one melee damage pick with ranged burst while retaining Varg's durable camp clear. Keep the alternative only if submitted gems improve across both sides and relevant maps. Then compare a separate **Tavi / Varg / Ren** version to test whether removing the linked pair itself helps under pressure. The local proxy is a screen; it is not a reproduction of hello's private bot.

For three-pick maps, choose a complete economic core by the second pick so the final pick can address a revealed threat. For four- and five-pick maps, retain independent camp coverage and spend only one later slot on a matchup response. Record the enemy's revealed draft and final allied roster in draft memory if gameplay needs to select an opening risk profile; do not overwrite the existing roster format without updating its readers.

## Required experiment order

### Phase 0: Preserve and instrument

1. Confirm `versions/03_d1_15_side_rosters` matches the current active source or preserve a fresh immutable copy before edits.
2. Build unchanged D1 and run a smoke match at the correct fuel cap.
3. Add sampled telemetry with no policy change.
4. Run both sides of Expanse, Collision and Interlock against G3 and compare statistics with the frozen D1.15 files.
5. If instrumentation changes outcomes or causes fuel problems, reduce it before strategy work.

### Phase 1: Economy challenger

1. Add one camp-selection improvement only.
2. Screen Expanse, Lanes and Monolith on both sides against G3 and the economic farmer proxy.
3. Inspect replays and telemetry for target churn, duplicate travel, abandoned captures, camp deaths and tiny-bank trips.
4. Expand to all nine maps and both sides only after the focused screen improves submitted gems.

### Phase 2: Carry conversion challenger

1. Start from the accepted economy challenger.
2. Add the narrow one-extra-cycle continuation condition.
3. Compare submitted gems, collected-to-submitted conversion, carried gems lost on death and travel turns.
4. Reject it if collected gems rise while submitted margin falls.

### Phase 3: Contact challenger

1. Start from the best economy/conversion parent.
2. Add one threat or PvP ability condition at a time.
3. Screen Collision and Interlock against the carrier-hunter and altar-denial proxies.
4. Ensure open-map farming does not regress.

### Phase 4: Draft challenger

1. Keep gameplay, purchases and the map/side first-pick prior fixed. Change one later pick in one opponent-pattern branch.
2. Verify the branch can actually observe the earlier enemy pick and cannot see a simultaneous pick. Check three-, four- and five-pick maps, both sides, legal duplicate-across-teams picks and draft-memory encoding.
3. Compare the same gameplay with and without the draft change against fixed local draft archetypes, including Cassi & Issac / Stellar / Ren. Count wins, own submitted gems, enemy submitted gems and linked-pair deaths.
4. Check unrelated archetypes and maps for losses before keeping the branch. Avoid promoting from one deterministic replay or an apparent counter interaction the active policy never executes.
5. Ask the user to conduct any official upload or unranked participation required for the final matchup verdict, as specified in `docs/strategy/prompt.md`. Analyse the resulting replays and match data.

### Phase 5: Live validation

1. Package the best candidate as D1.16 with a clear version marker.
2. Preserve its exact source before upload.
3. `docs/strategy/prompt.md` reserves all official uploads and participation for the user. Give the user the exact local package and evidence for manual upload; do not upload it yourself.
4. After the user makes the candidate active, ask them to run the five-map unranked groups against the same five opponents when available. Analyse those results. The site permits a limited number of unranked games per hour; request this only for a stable candidate, not every local experiment.
5. Inspect at least one economic loss and one combat loss in the visualizer before the next change.
6. Record every match group ID, roster, map, side, score, kills, camps, gold, deaths and observed failure mode.

## Promotion gates

### Minimum engineering gate

- Builds successfully.
- Completes the full local map/side matrix at `--fuel 100000` with zero bot faults.
- No observed fuel suspension or telemetry-induced action loss.
- Frozen D1.15 and G3 sources remain unchanged.
- Candidate source and results are archived with an unambiguous version name.

### Local evidence gate

Local results are a regression screen, not the competitive verdict. D1.15 scored 16/18 against G3, but then went 0/25 live. Prefer a candidate that preserves at least 12/18 against G3 and improves the relevant pressure/economy metrics, but do not discard a promising live-specialized candidate solely because it gives back a few G3 wins.

### Live D1.16 gate

Promote D1.16 when a comparable 25-map current-opponent suite meets these conditions:

- at least **5 map wins out of 25**;
- zero compile, runtime or fuel faults;
- meaningful submitted-gem improvement over D1.15's 6,816 aggregate;
- the wins are not all explained by opponent faults;
- at least one replay shows the intended policy causing the improvement.

Stretch targets are one match-group win, then two. If the first live candidate remains 0–25, keep it only if its score distribution and replay evidence identify a clear next experiment.

## Decision discipline

- Verify the active import path before measuring any change.
- Change one causal mechanism per challenger.
- Diagnose with submitted gems, camps, gold, deaths and replay events together.
- Keep the existing native distance-field movement unless a replacement proves better.
- Cache static work and bound all per-turn searches.
- Default to independent farming. Regroup only around an observable high-value event.
- Optimize secured gems per action, including denied enemy gems when the denial is likely and cheap.
- Separate map economy, carry conversion, combat and drafting experiments so failures can be attributed.
- Archive rejected experiments with their evidence; do not allow abandoned variants to remain ambiguous active bots.
- Live matches against current teams are the final evidence. Local proxies are useful screens and can be badly misleading.
