# Camp selection and exploration

Sources: `objectives.cpp` in all variants; `route_plan.cpp` and `loop_planner.cpp` in `bots/leader_party_subhan` and the preferred `bots/leader_party_tuned`. Both precompute Subhan's camp loops at draft time; the untuned bot starts following them after two equipment phases and the tuned profile after three. They follow the next currently eligible planned camp, advance on completion or abandonment, and fall back to baseline selection when no route camp qualifies. Movement and actual banking altar selection remain unchanged. See the [planner comparison](../subhan_comparison.md) for the exact model and the [configuration evaluation](../config_tuning.md) for the complete combined configuration.

The original `leader_party` retains the distance baseline after the paired six-map comparison. One forward native field evaluates all candidates; unreachable or locally cooled-down groups are skipped. Known respawn timers are compared with estimated arrival. Unseen camp state remains unknown; design HP supplies a conservative fallback.

Distance mode scores 1/(travel+1), with a nearby-ally competition penalty. The alternative value router estimates (gems*300 + gold)/(approach + clear + capture + altar travel + tribute), using gold*10 during the first two equipment phases. Ratios are compared with 64-bit integer cross multiplication. Clear time is remaining/design HP divided by basic strength plus 20% focus. Capture is estimated at five rounds; altar geometry and tribute are approximations. No learned clear-time model is claimed. Both modes reject estimated solo clear costs above 90 to avoid enormous corner camps while allowing ambush gem camps. Neither model proves combat safety.

When candidate work is deferred, telemetry records deferral; it does not mark candidates unreachable. The current executor can attempt exploration while waiting for a planning budget. Exploration targets least recently observed camp approaches, with distance and stable-slot staggering, and reconsiders after eight rounds or three blocked turns. Generic unseen viewpoints are not implemented. Once a useful local task is selected it continues independently of leadership changes.
