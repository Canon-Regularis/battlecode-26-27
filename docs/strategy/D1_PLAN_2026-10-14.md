# D1: Resource-Cycle Bot — Research and Implementation Plan

**Status:** plan recorded before D1 gameplay implementation; implementation and first broad local evaluation complete.  
**Scope:** a new standalone local bot under `bots/D1/`; G2/G3, their snapshots, and the submission package remain untouched. No official submission or external participation.

## Objective

Maximize the rate at which the team converts camp rewards into permanently tributed gems, while reducing carried-gem loss and wasted travel. The primary decision metric is expected team gems secured per team-turn, including the cost of capture, travel, bank travel/channeling, respawn delay and a conservative risk penalty. Denial and combat are useful only when their expected score impact exceeds the farming cycle they displace.

The bot is intentionally centered on a small number of economic decisions. It will not begin with a large threat model, fixed map-wide routes, elaborate opponent prediction or speculative mechanics.

## Evidence and assumptions

- **RULE:** A team wins at 1,000 tributed gems. At the round limit, submitted gems are the first tiebreak; carried gems are exposed to champion kills, and camp deaths destroy them.
- **RULE:** A camp group pays only after all monsters are defeated and an ally holds an eligible spawn tile uncontested for five capture phases. More allies do not accelerate the timer; allies present at payout divide the reward.
- **RULE:** Altars require a channel; channel time grows with the bag at start, and the complete current bag is paid when the channel completes. Damage alone does not cancel it; stun, death and locks do.
- **RULE:** Vision is per champion. Camp metadata is available during gameplay, but dynamic camp state and champions remain visibility-limited. Team shared memory is only eight bytes and no atomic update is documented.
- **RULE:** `--fuel 100000` corresponds to 2,500,000 Python fuel for local bot runs.
- **INFERENCE:** The safest economic unit is a *complete cycle*: approach → clear → capture → approach altar → channel. Ranking by distance or raw camp gems alone can select a slow or unsafe target.
- **HYPOTHESIS:** A compact deterministic candidate scorer with local visibility-based risk bands will outperform fixed routes and layered prediction while remaining fuel-efficient. Results must establish this.
- **HYPOTHESIS:** A farmer should avoid long enemy-side excursions when the cycle’s travel and risk cost exceed a nearer alternative, even if the distant camp has a larger nominal reward.

## Decision model

### 1. Candidate camp cycle

For each camp group, build one candidate from documented static reward/geometry and currently visible dynamic state. Ignore a group known to be alive, respawning, occupied in a way that delays respawn, or unreachable; treat unknown dynamic state as uncertain rather than empty.

Estimate:

```text
cycle_turns = travel_to_attack_tile
            + estimated_clear_turns
            + capture_phases_remaining
            + travel_from_capture_to_safe_altar_range
            + tribute_channel_turns
            + expected_delay

effective_gems = estimated_reward_gems * solo_capture_share
               + expected_unbanked_gems_secured
               - risk_weight * expected_gems_lost

score = effective_gems / max(1, cycle_turns)
```

The initial estimator is deliberately bounded and coarse: legal SDK path distance, visible monster HP, champion base damage/kit estimates where available, five capture phases unless progress is observable, current bag size and altar distance. Apply a modest risk penalty for visible enemy/camp attack coverage, low health, enemy-side distance and an exposed long return. Do not claim calibrated death probabilities; the first implementation uses explicit conservative bands. Stable tie-breaking by group handle prevents oscillation.

If the SDK lacks a reliable group distance or a group's dynamic status is hidden, use shortest legal path to a valid attack tile/capture tile and mark status confidence low. Never path onto monster-occupied cells. Use the SDK movement masks and movement API for legal steps; recover from a hidden blocker returning `False` by recalculating from the updated observation.

### 2. Resource and capture behavior

- Attack visible camps while a profitable selected group is in range; select targets to reduce remaining group HP and avoid wasteful overkill where damage estimates are available.
- After clearing, occupy the safest free spawn tile and hold it through payout. Re-evaluate only on a material event: enemy contest, death/recall, reward payout, newly visible threat, or a clearly superior immediate opportunity. Do not abandon a nearly completed valuable capture for a speculative route.
- Prefer solo payout when possible. Join another ally's camp only when the time saved, safety or contest value exceeds both lost parallel coverage and reward dilution.
- Do not hard-partition camps by champion ID: earlier partition experiments regressed by restricting access to nearby profitable targets.

### 3. Banking and carrier safety

Choose an altar by legal path distance plus visible threat/lock penalty. Begin a tribute when the current bag's expected exposure while continuing the farm cycle exceeds the opportunity cost of the altar detour and channel. Urgency rises with bag size, low health, nearby visible opponents, dangerous enemy-side position, and either team's proximity to 1,000. A banked winning score is an immediate priority.

If no credible danger exists, allow a short high-value camp continuation when its incremental expected secured gems exceed the extra travel/channel exposure. This comparison is bag-aware and based on route/action estimates, not a fixed global gem threshold. Use recall only as recovery when its channel and lost farm tempo are justified; retain the bot's economic role rather than adding broad low-health retreat rules.

### 4. Limited denial and combat

Combat remains subordinate to conversion. Attack an enemy carrier when visible, reachable and a kill is high-confidence; interrupt an imminent winning tribute or valuable capture when the turn order and legal action make denial plausible. Avoid chasing into fog or away from a productive local cycle. Altar locks and speculative capture/tribute timing tricks are excluded from the initial policy; consider them only as isolated later experiments with a measured value case.

### 5. Coordination under partial information

Use visible allied positions and known occupancy to avoid duplicating a camp when another equally good unoccupied candidate exists. Do not assume team-wide vision, hidden inventory, atomic shared memory or direct gem transfer. The initial D1 version uses deterministic local selection plus a compact, versioned hint only if the eight-byte shared-memory semantics can be safely implemented and a benchmark shows value. Without reliable coordination, local behavior must remain valid and productive.

## Implementation shape

Create an independent bot package in `bots/D1/` with a minimal entry point and a small number of modules:

- `src/main.py`: draft/game lifecycle and safe turn boundary.
- `src/draft.py`: deterministic legal roster choice, kept simple until gathering performance is benchmarked.
- `src/d1.py`: observe → select economic mode → execute; no copied G3 policy.
- `src/economy.py`: camp cycle and bank-versus-farm scoring.
- `src/navigation.py`: legal target-tile/path helpers with bounded work and obstruction recovery.

Use the existing SDK/package contract as a reference for integration only. Author D1 policy independently. Avoid external dependencies and broad persistent route state. All computations must be bounded by map size, visible camps and altars; check fuel before optional scoring refinements and yield safely with a legal fallback when fuel is low.

## Research and build sequence

1. **Contract survey:** confirm package/build conventions, relevant Python SDK methods and CLI outputs. Resolve only facts needed for a runnable D1; preserve documented contradictions as uncertainty.
2. **Plan checkpoint:** this document is the required pre-code design checkpoint.
3. **Minimal economic baseline:** implement legal draft, visible camp clearing, capture holding and altar tribute in D1's own source. Build and run a short smoke match on contrasting maps.
4. **Cycle scorer:** add path/action/reward ranking, full-cycle bank cost and bounded risk bands. Compare against D1's own nearest-camp/simple-carry baseline.
5. **Survival and coordination ablations:** test only one compact addition at a time (e.g. safer altar choice, avoid visible contested group, ally duplication avoidance). Drop branches that do not improve aggregate outcomes.
6. **Evaluation and hardening:** test all nine maps and both sides against G3, a carrier-hunter proxy and altar-denial proxy where runner time permits. Use the true cap (`--fuel 100000`), record faults, wins, submitted/conceded gems, deaths and runtime/fuel evidence available from the runner. Preserve replay/result manifests. The runner exposes no seed flag, so fixed map/side matrices are comparative evidence, not independent random samples.

## Promotion and stopping criteria

D1 is considered *finished as a standalone challenger* when it builds cleanly, completes the agreed local evaluation without faults or fuel exhaustion, and its source, results and limitations are documented. It is not promoted over G3 solely because the algorithm is more sophisticated or its narrow pilot is favorable.

Local promotion requires repeatable aggregate improvement in secured gems or win outcomes across the broad map/side matrix, no unacceptable matchup collapse, no fuel/fault regression, and a review of representative losses. If D1 does not beat G3, retain it as an evaluated challenger and keep G3 canonical. No official submission is part of this goal.

## Risks and falsification

- **Scorer error:** turn estimates may misprice abilities, cooldowns, camp retaliation or channel timing. Falsify by examining predicted versus observed cycle times and payout.
- **Risk heuristic too cautious:** excessive risk penalties can strand D1 on low-yield local targets. Compare raw cycle score, own submitted gems and opponent submissions; remove unhelpful penalty terms.
- **Risk heuristic too aggressive:** distant camps may increase bag loss. Inspect deaths by location/carrying state and compare safe-route ablations.
- **Coordination complexity:** hidden allies and memory writers can cause stale or conflicting assignments. Begin without shared-memory dependency; add only with observed duplication evidence and robust fallback.
- **Benchmark noise:** no seed control means side/map-matched results can still be unpaired. Report exact sample size and avoid overstating causality.
- **Time and fuel:** scoring all group/attack positions repeatedly can waste fuel. Cache only per-turn static derivations if profiling demonstrates a need; prefer linear scans and SDK distance/path primitives before custom search.

## Decisions intentionally deferred

Champion composition, rich threat prediction, gold/item optimization, altar locks, map-specific circuits, and team memory are experiments rather than foundations. Each must earn inclusion through an ablation. User-reported top-team use of split gathering and regrouping is a useful hypothesis; D1 will first determine whether compact local assignment plus payout concentration improves its own measured resource cycle.

## Implementation and evaluation checkpoint — 2026-10-14

The first implementation followed the full-cycle scorer design. Its measured Packs pilots scored 188 and then 48 gems against G3, while a separate adaptive banking pilot scored 48. Because the scoring model did not establish an advantage, D1 was simplified to a path-aware nearest-camp/capture loop with immediate gem conversion. The first simple control scored 632 on Packs; adding visible carrier attacks, Varg's low-health shield, and attack-reset ordering then produced one full-cap Packs win (1,000–944).

The final 18-match fixed map/side matrix against G3 gave D1 **5 wins / 18**: both wins on Packs, both on Rows, and one on Elkring as Team B. G3 won the other 13. Two diagnostic losses were stark: on Collision, D1 submitted 0 to G3's 1,012; on Interlock with D1 as Team B, D1 submitted 144 to G3's 1,026. This confirms the tactical additions can win locally but the broader economy remains map-dependent. Results, match artifacts and limitations are summarized in [D1/RESULTS.md](../../bots/D1/RESULTS.md).

**Disposition:** D1 is a runnable, built and locally evaluated standalone challenger, not a promotion candidate. Keep G3 canonical. The next research step is diagnosing low-capture/failed-bank behavior on Collision, Corners, Expanse, Interlock, Lanes and Monolith before changing D1's policy again. The first cycle scorer source is retained at `bots/D1/experiments/cycle_scorer_v0_1.py`; no rejected prototype was deleted.
